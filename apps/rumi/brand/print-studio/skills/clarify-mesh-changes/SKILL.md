# Clarify mesh changes

Use ordinary language and the visible model to establish a shared reference. Use a highlight to visually ground region-specific edits and named parts. Follow clear requests, including undo and selections from choices you offered. Ask for clarification when the intended region or action is ambiguous; do not require a special confirmation phrase.

## Identify the region

If the spoken request includes a `cursor-at-start-of-spoken-request` reference, use that image and region for its pointing words. It was requested at the first transcript chunk, not at exact audio onset; compare its request and capture times. Do not replace it with wherever the cursor moved later. If it failed or is ambiguous, ask for a new reference. Otherwise, when the person refers to the region they are pointing at, call `viewer_cursor_snapshot` to ask the open viewer for a fresh capture. Cursor mesh vision is blue; agent-proposed regions are teal. A captured result contains an image, capture time, pointed part, world-space Gaussian sphere, and model identity. An editable-model hit also includes `region.modelLocalBlob`: pass it as one blob to `viewer_highlight`, copying `region.selection` too when present, with `identity.design.path`, `revision`, and `url`, to show your own proposed region. Do not move the camera before taking the snapshot. The capture occurs when the viewer processes the request, through an immediate viewer wakeup; it does not recover where the cursor was when the person spoke. On `no_hit`, ask them to keep the pointer over the intended surface and retry. On `viewer_unavailable`, ask them to open the 3D preview. On `stale_view` or `capture_failed`, resolve the viewer issue and capture again; never substitute an earlier region. A snapshot does not approve an edit.

Read `mesh_state` and inspect the selected model's source before choosing coordinates. Whenever you refer to a part such as a chin, jaw, cheek, eye, rim, or crease, first show that part with `viewer_highlight`. Explain it as the teal highlighted area with a simple positional description. Do not assume the person knows anatomical or modelling terms. For several distinct parts, highlight and discuss one at a time; a combined highlight is appropriate only when the person is choosing the combined edit region. Named parts can help identify a region, but one mesh may contain many meaningful features. Describe left and right from the person's current view or use an unambiguous feature; do not silently switch between the wearer's and viewer's perspective.

For an editable model loaded with `viewer_load`, call `viewer_highlight` with the exact `design.path`, `design.revision`, and `design.url` from `mesh_state`. Give the region a short descriptive label. Supply a few ellipsoidal blobs with `center`, `radii`, and `rotationDegrees`, each a three-number array. Coordinates are millimetres in the model's local frame, after part transforms but before whole-design placement. Rotation uses XYZ Euler degrees. Radii specify nominal extent, not diameter or Gaussian standard deviation. The viewer blends their influence with a fixed soft fade and reveals wireframe within the region.

Start with the smallest collection that covers the intended area. A flattened, tilted blob can cover an eyebrow; two blobs can indicate both eyes without covering the entire forehead. Inspect depth as well as width and height: a blob can also intersect the back of a thin shell. This is an approximate conversational region, not a precise edit mask or a selection of entire connected parts.

The tool waits for a matching receipt from the visible viewer. Only `rendered` confirms rendering; `not_rendered` or `superseded` does not. On failure, explain that the highlight is not confirmed, ask the person to open the preview, and retry. Never say “I have highlighted it” before a `rendered` result. Rendering still does not establish that the person saw or approved it. Use their reply in context, including corrections or a choice you offered. On `stale_model`, read the state again and recompute against the new source; resolve build errors first. Highlights are temporary and clear when the source revision changes or another model loads. Imported STL objects do not support this tool.

## Establish the change

Ask one short question in the conversation about the uncertainty that matters, then wait for a spoken or typed reply. Do not use a questionnaire or require clicking an option. For an eye region, distinguish enlarging the openings from widening the surrounding material. Establish whether bigger means wider, taller, thicker, or more raised when the context does not settle it. Do not infer exact edit boundaries from the soft highlight.

When the person corrects the region, stop work based on the previous region, replace the complete blob list, and clarify any remaining ambiguity. Keep their requested change in context. Do not make unrelated improvements while waiting. Hiding the highlight is a viewing preference, not approval. Confirmation of a region is not authorization for an unrelated change or a print.

When the requested change is clear, edit the model source and inspect the resulting preview and measurements. Clear an obsolete reference with `blobs: []`, using the current model identity and an empty label. Explain changes using the person's feature names rather than coordinates or triangle indices. Only refer to labels or viewer controls that actually exist; do not invent hover labels.

Cursor driven mesh highlighting uses local patch, connected surface, whole part, and boundary edge scopes. A captured `region.selection` identifies the exact part path and geometric surface for this source revision; copy it with `modelLocalBlob` for teal confirmation. Surface boundaries follow creases, material seams, and disconnected pieces, not inferred anatomical names. Short voice phrases such as “whole panel”, “whole part”, “just the edge”, “larger selection”, and “smaller selection” refine the original pointing reference locally. Use the refined attached snapshot; never substitute a later hover. For bilateral changes, inspect the model’s actual symmetry and highlight both intended regions; do not assume its symmetry axis or silently treat one side as both. Selection phrases change presentation only and never authorize an unrelated geometry edit.
